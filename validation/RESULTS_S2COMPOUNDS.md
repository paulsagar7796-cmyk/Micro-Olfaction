# System 2+ on discrete compounds: results

This reports the test registered in [PROTOCOL_S2COMPOUNDS.md](PROTOCOL_S2COMPOUNDS.md) (commit
4c33b4c): System 2+ on five independent real Figaro sensor arrays (UCI 361, "Twin gas sensor arrays"),
classifying four chemically fixed compounds (ethanol, methane, ethylene, carbon monoxide), each board
held out in turn. Numbers come from `results/system2_compounds*.csv` and
`results/logs/system2_compounds.log`, regenerated by `./reproduce.sh twin_arrays system2_compounds`
(about 1 minute).

**Protocol timeline:** protocol written before any classification; one full run preceded the commit
(disclosed in the protocol's Deviations, with its numbers). After that run, three places where the code
did not match the registered text were fixed to match it, and HD1 turned out to be unattainable as
written. The fixes were not result-driven: one of them made the comparison harder for System 2+, since
the random forest, trained as registered, rose from 90.1% to 98.9%. System 2+ itself moved from 95.1%
to 95.4%.

## Bottom line

- **System 2+ works on real sensors when the targets are fixed chemicals.** It classifies trials from a
  board it was never trained on with **95.4% accuracy** (chance 25%), calibrated on 20 of that board's
  trials. On the home recordings, where wine and banana change composition, it scored 43% balanced
  accuracy. This supports the view that the home test measured an unstable target, not a failure of the
  method ([RESULTS_S2HOME.md](RESULTS_S2HOME.md)).
- **Adaptation rescued the one board where transfer failed, and improved every board's average.** On board 5, the model
  trained on the other four boards reached only 58%. Aligned and calibrated on 20 of board 5's trials,
  System 2+ reached **90%**. On the other four boards, where the unadapted model already transferred well
  (91-96%), adaptation added a small but consistent 1.4-2.8 points. The typical gain is therefore about
  2.6 points (the median); the mean, 8.1, mostly reflects board 5.
- **HD1 is formally not met.** Adaptation improved accuracy on 5 of 5 held-out boards; with 5 boards,
  no two-sided test can reach p < 0.05. Registering a criterion that could not be met was a design error
  in the protocol, recorded in its Deviations, not a weak result.
- **Pseudo-labels add nothing here** (95.4% with, 95.3% without), as on the home recordings.
- **A random forest given the same 20 calibration trials does better: 98.9%.** System 2+ is not the most
  accurate classifier on this data. Its case remains cost and online operation: a linear readout that
  adapts sample by sample for about 1 µJ per inference on a Cortex-M4 (paper, section 5), where a
  100-tree forest retrained with each board's calibration trials is a different class of device load.
  That comparison of cost was not part of this test.

## Data

Five boards of eight Figaro sensors (TGS2611, TGS2612, TGS2610, TGS2602, each at 5.65 V and 5.00 V
heater voltage), each tested on its own days, 640 trials: boards 1-3 with 4 repetitions per condition
(160 trials each), boards 4-5 with 2 (80 each). Each trial is a 600-s pulse: baseline, exposure from
about 57 s, plateau, recovery. Features: 64 raw (steady-state and three-timescale EMA transients per
sensor, Vergara et al. 2012's method recomputed from the traces) plus 64 log-pattern features.

## Registered results

Accuracy on each held-out board's trials after its 20 calibration trials, mean ± standard deviation over
5 boards × 15 seeds:

| Method | Accuracy | CO | Ethanol | Ethylene | Methane |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **System 2+** | **0.954 ± 0.047** | 0.919 | 0.981 | 0.919 | 0.996 |
| System 2+ without pseudo-labels | 0.953 ± 0.047 | 0.913 | 0.981 | 0.920 | 0.996 |
| System 2 (no alignment or calibration) | 0.872 ± 0.151 | 0.820 | 0.828 | 0.850 | 0.991 |
| Random forest (source + the 20 calibration trials) | 0.989 ± 0.009 | 1.000 | 0.982 | 0.980 | 0.994 |

Per-gas columns are recall. The classes are balanced, so accuracy equals balanced accuracy.

The ± is a descriptive spread over the 75 runs (5 boards × 15 seeds), mostly the gap between board 5
and the rest; it is not a confidence interval. The calibration trials are fixed, so the seeds change
only the order in which SGD sees the samples: they are not independent replicates. The unit of
replication is the board (N = 5), and the per-board table below is the evidence. Every run is in
`results/system2_compounds_seeds.csv`.

**Per board** (mean over seeds):

| Held-out board | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| System 2+ | 0.983 | 0.978 | 0.923 | 0.983 | 0.900 |
| System 2 | 0.957 | 0.956 | 0.909 | 0.956 | 0.584 |
| Difference | +0.026 | +0.022 | +0.014 | +0.028 | **+0.316** |

**Board 5 is the result that matters.** It was tested late in the campaign (days 18 and 22) and, like
board 4, has two repetitions per condition. The model trained on the other four boards transfers poorly
to it (58%), and alignment plus calibration on 20 of its trials recover most of the loss (90%). On
boards 1-4 the unadapted model already transfers well, and adaptation adds 1.4-2.8 points.

**System 2+ confusion** (mean counts over seeds, calibration trials excluded):

| True \ predicted | CO | Ethanol | Ethylene | Methane |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CO | 128.1 | 0.1 | 1.2 | 5.5 |
| Ethanol | 1.1 | 133.0 | 0.8 | 0.1 |
| Ethylene | 0.3 | 0.0 | 121.3 | 13.3 |
| Methane | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.7 | 134.3 |

The main error is ethylene read as methane: both are small hydrocarbons to which these hydrocarbon
sensors (TGS2610, 2611, 2612) respond similarly.

**HD1 — does adaptation help? Not met as registered.** Adaptation improved accuracy on 5 of 5 held-out
boards; with 5 boards, no two-sided test can reach p < 0.05 (the registered Wilcoxon gives 0.0625, its
minimum). The rule's other condition, a mean gain of at least 5 points, was met (+8.1), but mostly
because of board 5.

**HD2 — is accuracy useful? Met** (0.954 against 0.80).

**Seed consistency and stream order** (added in the pre-v4 audit; `results/system2_compounds_exploratory.csv`):

| Held-out board | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seeds where System 2+ beats System 2 | 13/15 | 13/15 | 10/15 | 12/15 | 15/15 |
| System 2+, registered order (sorted by gas) | 0.983 | 0.978 | 0.923 | 0.983 | 0.900 |

- **Board-level improvements are averages.** On boards 1-4, where the gain is 1.4-2.8 points,
  adaptation beat the unadapted model in 10-13 of the 15 seeds, not in all of them. On board 5 it won
  in all 15.
- **The rescue does not depend on the sorted order.** The registered order streams each board's trials
  sorted by gas, an order that depends on the labels. Streamed in a random order instead, System 2+
  gives the same picture: board 5 reaches 88.3% against 90.0%, and the other boards move by at most
  0.6 points.

## Caveats

- **Protocol timeline.** Protocol written before any classification; one full run preceded the commit
  (disclosed). It should be described that way, not as pre-registered.
- **No genuine time stream.** The archive does not say which day each repetition was recorded on, so
  trials within a board are streamed in a declared (gas, level, repetition) order. Alignment and
  pseudo-labelling therefore run on an artificial order; what the test measures is adaptation to a new
  board, not to drift over time within one. A random order gives the same result (Exploratory).
- **Easier than the drift dataset in some ways.** Four classes instead of six, one laboratory chamber,
  and boards tested within about three weeks, against the drift dataset's 36 months.
- **The feature extraction is this project's implementation** of Vergara et al.'s published method, not
  their code.

## What this means

System 2+'s learning, unchanged, transfers to an independent real sensor array: 95% accuracy on boards
it never saw. Adaptation improved every board's average, and on the one board where the unadapted model
failed it recovered accuracy from 58% to 90%. The home-recordings result is consistent with the target
variability hypothesis: on fixed chemicals the same pipeline works. With different sensors and a
laboratory chamber, this supports the hypothesis rather than proving it. For the architecture, classifying chemically defined gases is supported; recognising
complex, changing household odours remains an open problem that needs its own data. A random forest
does better still on this data, so System 2+'s claim should rest on accuracy that is competitive at a
microcontroller's cost, not on being the most accurate classifier.
